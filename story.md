# 40 Days Under: Story

*A survival story about one engineer living 15 meters under the Detroit River while the city above him is gone.*

Sources: `docs/Narrative/WorldBible.md`, `docs/Narrative/OpeningSlice.md`, `docs/Narrative/Act2Branches.md`.
Those files are the canonical detail. This page is the plot in one place.

---

## Premise

An outbreak has overrun metro Detroit. The infected can't swim, so the river is the one place they can't
follow. Thousands of their bodies still drift on the current, and from below they form a shifting, groaning
mat that blocks the sunlight: **the living ceiling**.

Under it, anchored 15 meters below the river near Belle Isle and the Renaissance Center, is **Lab 04**, a
refitted benthic research station. Christian Thomas was already inside it, mid-contract, when the city fell.
He has a diesel compressor for air, a desalinator, a pantry, and a supply boat due in three weeks.

He also can't simply leave. After living at depth his body is saturated with nitrogen, and any ascent costs
**17 hours of decompression**. The surface is close, and he can't safely get to it.

## The protagonist

**Christian Thomas**, 28, a Detroit-born software and AI engineer who builds autonomous marine robots (AUVs
and ROVs). He was contracted to upgrade Lab 04's telemetry and sensor arrays, so he knows its hull, its valves
and its patched-together firmware better than anyone alive.

He isn't a soldier or a hero. He's an engineer talking himself steady: dry, specific and quiet. He narrates his
days into a log recorder and talks to the station as if it could hear him. A taped photo of his family on the
People Mover curls in the humidity on the wall.

## The rules of his world

Survival is arithmetic, and the numbers are always in front of him:

| Resource | Numbers |
| :--- | :--- |
| Air | A diesel compressor tethered to a hidden surface buoy. **2000 L of diesel, burning 2 L an hour**, runs dry around day 41. When the fuel goes, the scrubbers fail and the CO2 climbs. |
| Water | The desalinator makes **20 L a day**, and it draws a lot of power. |
| Food | **700 canned meals.** |
| Power | A solar buoy, weak in storms and weaker when bodies drift over the panels. |
| Ascent | **17 hours** of decompression before he can surface without the bends. |

There's no screen HUD in his world, and none in the game. He reads everything off physical things: the gauges
on the wall manifold, the dive watch on his left wrist, the projection inside his mask.

## Act 1: The routine (Days 1-20)

**Day 1, the opening.** Christian wakes to the thump of the pump. *Still running.* He walks his morning
rounds:

1. He reads the manifold: pressure holding, scrubber in the green. *Boring is good.*
2. He checks his watch: fifteen meters, nitrogen climbing as it does every hour, seventeen hours to
   decompress. *Not today.*
3. He records the day's log: the diesel, the water, the 700 meals. *Math is for later.*
4. The station reports a fault: hydrophone array, channel three, no signal. He wrote that firmware. He
   reseats the bus and clears the fault. *Don't take it personally.*
5. The intake valve has seized, probably zebra mussels, so he cranks it free by hand. *Nobody's timing me.*
6. He makes himself look up through the acrylic dome, just once. Hundreds of silhouettes drift against
   the pale green light. *They can't come down.*
7. He closes the log. Lab 04 is stable. *The supply boat comes in three weeks. I just have to keep the
   lights on until then.*

The rest of Act 1 is the same rhythm: maintenance, patching the hull, clearing the intake, debugging
telemetry, and scouting the rusted Great Lakes freighter wrecks nearby for salvage with a speargun and
scavenged ROV parts. The days are quiet and the tone is calm, domestic and a little funny. The horror stays
overhead.

## Act 2: The boat that doesn't come (Days 21-40)

**Day 21.** Christian waits at the viewport with the radio, and the supply boat doesn't arrive.
*Boats get late. I'll check again at dusk.* At dusk: *Nothing. Okay. We ration.*

He cuts his food and water. The isolation starts to work on him: phantom chatter on dead frequencies, a
tally of days scratched by the viewport by the station's last occupant that stops at 12. The fuel gauge keeps
falling.

**Day 40.** The hydrophone array catches a weak broadcast through the static. It's a woman's voice from
the trawler *Marguerite Anne*: forty feet long, two kilometers upriver toward Belle Isle, a family of four,
**full diesel tanks**. They can't stay where they are. They don't know Lab 04 exists.

*Full tanks. They don't know we're here.*

Christian has to choose.

## Branch A: Broadcast

He transmits Lab 04's coordinates and acoustic beacon. *I'm telling a stranger where I sleep.* The signal
can't be unsent, and the station's stealth is gone for good.

- **The family docks.** The trawler ties up over Lab 04, the family comes through the airlock, and the diesel
  is transferred. Now the station's arithmetic has to stretch: 20 L of water a day and 700 meals, split
  between more people. There's paranoia, an argument over the airlock hatch, maybe a mutiny. When drifting
  bodies swarm the trawler's hull, everyone defends it together.
  *"We brought fuel. We didn't bring them." / "Nobody brings them. They just come."*
- **The siege.** The beacon drew the current-borne horde straight onto the station. Christian has to hold
  the bulkheads and the airlock for **48 hours**. *Hull's holding. It's loud, that's all.*

**Ending: Rescued.** At hour 48 an automated aerial sweep passes overhead and finds them. It's quiet and
flat, with no triumph. *Engines. Not ours. ...Somebody's still out there.*

**Ending: Scuttled.** If the station can't hold, Christian floods the compartments and they escape upriver
on the trawler. *Sorry. You were a good house.* The river closes over Lab 04.

## Branch B: Go alone (the RiverWalk run)

He keeps radio silence and goes for the fuel himself. First he prepares, in any order:

1. **Emergency decompression.** He bleeds the nitrogen down on a compressed, dangerous schedule.
   *Seventeen hours is a long time. I'm squeezing it.*
2. **A 600 m tether hose.** He builds a weighted air-and-tether line from salvaged ROV umbilical.
   *Don't kink it.*
3. **A decoy beacon.** He programs an acoustic lure to pull the drifting bodies away from the marina.
   *Go listen to something else.*

- **The swim.** Six hundred meters upriver to the Milliken State Park harbor and the RiverWalk fuel dock,
  hidden under the living ceiling, with a tethered micro-ROV scouting ahead on sonar. *Lights off. Let the
  river do the work.*
- **The siphon.** He drains diesel from listing, unstable boats at the docks. One loud splash and the
  dockside swarm comes. *Slow hands. One drop at a time.*
- **The return.** He hauls the fuel back along the tether and reseals the hatch.
  *Pressure's back. Lights are on. I'm home.*

**Ending: Alone.** He's safe and powered, and completely alone. The pump thumps, the lights hold, and the
ceiling drifts past the dome. His last log entry: *I'm okay. I'm alone. Both of those are true.*

## Death

Death is permanent: hypoxia when the air goes bad, the bends from surfacing too fast, or a swarm. It's never
shown as gore. There's a held breath, the pump fading out, then black, and the save is gone.

## What the story is about

- **Isolation as a survival stat.** Loneliness is tracked like air and water, and it wears him down the
  same way.
- **Engineering as coping.** Christian survives by fixing things and narrating them. The routine is the
  armour.
- **Trust as a cost.** Branch A trades safety and stealth for other people. Branch B keeps both and pays
  in solitude.
- **The city overhead.** Detroit is always present but out of reach: the Renaissance Center through the
  fog, the Ambassador Bridge, the Windsor skyline, the RiverWalk lamps still flickering.

**Tone:** restrained, grounded and lonely. The horror is atmosphere and pressure: silhouettes against green
light and the sound of the hull. Never close-up gore.
